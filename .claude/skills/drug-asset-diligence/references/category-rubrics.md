# Category rubrics

For each category: what the paragraph must establish, what does not count as evidence, and what to
write when the evidence is missing. Labels are fixed and never change.

---

## Target

Establish the molecular target, the site and nature of the intervention, the mechanism as
understood, and the strength of human validation. Name what class precedent exists and whether it
transfers to this molecule.

Does not count as clinical validation: target expression in diseased tissue, genetic association,
knockout or transgenic model phenotypes, pathway plausibility, or a compelling mechanistic story.
Class precedent validates the class, not the molecule's selectivity, exposure, or safety.

Where pharmacology is undisclosed — an unnamed receptor, an unspecified binding site, an
unquantified selectivity claim — say so plainly and make disclosure a priority request. Do not
substitute the disease gene for the drug target, and do not infer a target from indication.

---

## Modality

Establish modality, formulation, route, dose, and schedule, each only as far as a cited source
supports. State what is proposed versus what has been administered to humans.

Cover the feasibility issues that belong to this modality: delivery to the compartment that
matters, manufacturing and CMC maturity, comparability across lots or processes, scale and cost of
goods, supply and second-source risk, stability and handling, device or administration burden, and
for biologics, immunogenicity risk. Where a detail properly belongs in the clinical or non-clinical
paragraph, put it there rather than duplicating it.

Do not invent undisclosed formulation, dose, or route. For an asset moving to a new compartment or
population, do not assume the existing formulation transfers.

---

## Competitive landscape

Establish today's standard of care, the approved competitors, the emerging competitors by stage and
sponsor, and the failures that tell you something about the indication. Then ask the only question
that matters: at launch, against that field, why would a prescriber, payer, or patient choose this?

Test differentiation on each axis that could carry it: efficacy, safety and tolerability, dosing
convenience, delivery, patient selection, durability, and access or price. A modest efficacy edge
against a cheap generic is not differentiation.

Cross-trial comparison is not head-to-head evidence. When you compare programs, name the
differences that could explain the gap: indication and line of therapy, population and baseline
severity, prior treatment, control arm, endpoint definition, timing of assessment, follow-up
duration, analysis population, and missing-data handling.

First-in-class and best-in-class are claims that require evidence. Attribute them to the sponsor
when the sponsor makes them, and say what would establish or refute them. "No approved therapy"
claims are checked against actual labels — including older, broadly worded, or symptomatic
indications.

Position the asset against the field it will meet at launch, not the field the deck was written
against.

---

## Commercial

Disease market size and asset revenue are different quantities, and conflating them is the most
common failure in this category. Work the funnel and show it. Detail in
`references/commercial-model.md`.

The paragraph should establish: the population actually reachable, the territory and rights on
offer, the pricing and payer environment, plausible uptake and persistence, treatment duration,
launch timing relative to competitors, and the development obligations attached to the opportunity.

Label sponsor forecasts as sponsor forecasts. Exclude unsupported expansion indications from the
base case and treat them as separate optionality with their own development cost. Where a market
figure in a supplied document is attached to a different indication, comparator, or geography, say
so and do not reuse it.

CMS charges, payments, spending, utilization, prices, and manufacturer revenue are not
interchangeable. When you use any of them, state the population, denominator, geography, reporting
lag, and coverage gaps.

Do not apply a full year of revenue to every incident case. Do not attach a precise probability of
commercial success you cannot derive.

---

## IP

Distinguish, explicitly: applications from grants; a patent family from a single member; the
jurisdictions where protection exists from those where it was merely sought; ownership from
license; claim scope from subject matter; and patent term from regulatory exclusivity.

Check coverage of the exact thing being bought — the specific compound, salt or polymorph, antibody
sequence or CDRs, active metabolite, formulation, and intended method of use. A patent family found
by searching the target or the sponsor is a candidate family, not verified asset coverage.

Use official registers for any legal-status conclusion. Patent counts do not establish
defensibility. A limited search does not establish freedom to operate, and nothing you do
establishes FTO — that is a counsel opinion.

Label expiry and runway estimates as tentative and name their dependencies: term adjustments and
extensions, supplementary protection certificates, pediatric extensions, terminal disclaimers,
maintenance status, and the expected approval date the runway is measured from. Orphan designation
is neither approval nor patent protection.

Request the claim chart, the granted-country schedule, the license and sublicense obligations
including royalties, consents, diligence terms and field or territory limits, and any reversion
documentation. Refer legal conclusions to patent counsel.

Apply an IP-runway requirement only if the user supplied one.

## Clinical

Establish stage and trial identifiers first, then design: randomization, blinding, control,
arms and sizes, endpoints and their definitions, analysis populations, statistical plan,
multiplicity handling, and missing-data rules.

Then the results: effect sizes with their uncertainty, the control arm's performance, absolute as
well as relative effects, discontinuations and their reasons, safety by system and severity,
follow-up duration, and generalizability to the population you would treat. Denominators are
mandatory — a percentage without its denominator is not a result.

Distinguish, every time: a registered plan, an ongoing study, a sponsor announcement, posted
registry results, a peer-reviewed publication, and a complete study report. Sponsor-reported
figures from a small trial are exactly that.

Registry presence, scientific advice, a designation, an independent monitoring committee's
recommendation to continue, and biomarker movement do not establish efficacy or regulatory
acceptance. A futility or safety review asks whether continuing is justified, not whether the
endpoint was met.

Separate monotherapy evidence from combination evidence; a combination signal does not reveal the
asset's independent contribution. Do not extrapolate across indications, lines, populations, or
compartments without saying that is what you are doing.

Statistical significance is not evidence of meaningful benefit. State what magnitude would be
clinically meaningful and whether the observed effect reaches it.

Assess the registration-enabling path: what trial would support approval, on what endpoint, against
what control, with what enrollment feasibility, at what cost and duration, and with what regulatory
precedent. A proposed pathway — single-arm, accelerated approval, surrogate endpoint — is a
hypothesis until an agency has agreed to it in writing. Ask for the written feedback, not the
sponsor's characterization of it.

---

## Non-clinical

Assess, as relevant to the asset: model relevance to human disease, reproducibility and independent
replication, blinding and randomization in the models, functional selectivity in cells rather than
only biochemical potency, PK and PD, exposure-response, bridging from model exposure to achievable
human unbound exposure, engagement in the tissue that matters, active metabolites, toxicology
including chronic and where relevant juvenile studies, immunogenicity, drug interactions, and
therapeutic margins.

The three inferences to refuse: biochemical potency is not clinical selectivity; systemic exposure
is not tissue engagement; an animal or biomarker effect is not durable patient benefit.

Challenge surrogate-endpoint and disease-modification claims directly. Ask what else could move the
surrogate, and whether the proposed mechanism itself could move it without clinical benefit.
Persistence after washout is more persuasive when parent drug and active metabolites have
demonstrably cleared — ask for the PK that supports it.

State the limits of translational analogies rather than letting a comparison to another program or
another species carry the argument.

## Recommendations for next step

Give a conditional, stage-appropriate decision, and define what it does and does not commit. For
screening work, "advance" typically means request confidential information and run a bounded expert
review — say so, so that no one reads it as a decision to invest.

Then the prioritized list: documents to request, analyses to run, experiments to commission,
questions to put to named kinds of expert, and development changes to negotiate. Order by impact on
the decision, degree of uncertainty, and how cheaply the uncertainty can be resolved. Put the check
that could kill the asset first, not the check that is easiest.

Separate fatal flaws (would end the opportunity), manageable risks (have a known mitigation and a
cost), and unknowns (no evidence either way yet). Missing evidence is not a negative finding — say
what would produce it.

No unconditional endorsement. No forced binary where the honest answer is "advance on these
conditions" or "hold pending these two documents".

---

## Rationale

Connect the recommendation to the evidence in one tight paragraph: what drives it, how confident
you are and why, the strongest argument against your position, what must be true for the asset to
succeed, and the specific findings that would change your conclusion.

The strongest counterargument is written to persuade, not to be dismissed. If the counterargument
is strong and unresolved, the recommendation should reflect that rather than overriding it.

Apply eligibility, mandate, or investment criteria only when the user supplied them, and name them
when you do. Criteria from an example, a prior engagement, or an older strategy document are not
in force unless the user says they are.
