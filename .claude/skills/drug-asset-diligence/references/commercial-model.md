# Commercial modelling

Disease market size is not asset revenue. The scorecard's Commercial paragraph must show the path
from one to the other, or state plainly that no defensible asset-specific forecast can be built
from the available evidence. Both are acceptable answers; a borrowed market figure is not.

## The funnel

Work it in this order, naming the source and year for each input:

1. **Incidence or prevalence** in the territory being offered — and say which one, because they
   answer different questions. Chronic therapy models prevalence; acute or single-course therapy
   models incidence.
2. **Diagnosed** — the fraction actually identified in routine care.
3. **Eligible** — by subtype, biomarker, line of therapy, severity, age, performance status, and
   the label you expect rather than the label you want.
4. **Reachable** — within the specialist infrastructure, testing, administration setting, and
   geography the product requires.
5. **Treated** — after uptake against incumbents, and after the share the asset can plausibly hold
   at its launch position.
6. **Revenue** — treated patients × net price per unit of therapy × actual duration, adjusted for
   persistence, discontinuation, and where relevant gross-to-net and mandatory rebates.

State units at every step: patients, courses, vials, days of therapy, or annualized patients. Show
the formula. Show the year the model runs to and the launch year it assumes.

## Common errors to refuse

- Applying a full year of revenue to every incident case. Short survival and short courses
  constrain revenue even when urgency is high.
- Percentage-of-market reasoning ("1% of a $40B market") in place of a population model.
- Reusing a market figure attached to a different indication, comparator, geography, or year.
  Check what the number in the source is actually a number *of*.
- Adding an expansion indication's revenue to the base case without its own development program,
  cost, and timeline.
- Treating list price as net price, or ignoring payer step-through, prior authorization, testing
  requirements, and site-of-care restrictions.
- Assuming the sponsor's uptake curve. Uptake against an entrenched, cheap, or well-tolerated
  incumbent is slow, and the launch-era field is not today's field.
- Carrying a precise-looking peak sales number forward once it has been labelled unverified.

## CMS and payer data

CMS charges, allowed amounts, payments, spending, utilization, prices, and manufacturer revenue are
distinct quantities. Never substitute one for another. Whenever a CMS figure is used, state:

- Which dataset, which year, and which version
- The population it covers — fee-for-service Medicare is not all Medicare, and Medicare is not the
  US market; Medicaid, commercial, 340B, and cash are separate
- The denominator: beneficiaries, claims, services, or providers
- The geography and the reporting lag between the service year and publication
- Known coverage gaps, suppression rules for small cells, and whether rebates are reflected —
  in most public CMS spending data they are not

Use CMS data for what it is good at: real-world utilization, treated-population size, site of care,
and price realism in a defined population. Do not use it as a proxy for global revenue.

## Sensitivities

Give a range, not a point. Vary the two or three inputs that actually move the answer — usually
eligible fraction, share, price, and duration — and state which one dominates. Where the honest
range spans an order of magnitude, say that, and say which evidence would narrow it.

Do not attach a probability of technical, regulatory, or commercial success you cannot derive from
a stated basis. If a risk-adjusted figure is required, state the basis class and its source, and
present the unadjusted number alongside it.

## Rights, obligations, and timing

Revenue only accrues where rights exist. Establish the territories and indications actually on
offer, field limits, sublicensing rights, royalty and milestone obligations flowing upstream, and
any development obligations attached — a late-stage global registration program is a cost, not a
benefit, if the buyer must fund it.

Launch timing interacts with all of the above: an asset that reaches the market after a superior
competitor, or shortly before loss of exclusivity on its own protection, has a materially different
commercial case than the same asset launching first.

## Labelling in the scorecard

In the Commercial paragraph, mark every sponsor figure as a sponsor estimate or scenario, mark your
own model as a model with its inputs, and name the single input that most determines the answer.
Where the evidence does not support a forecast, write what would have to be supplied to build one —
that is the useful output at screening stage.
