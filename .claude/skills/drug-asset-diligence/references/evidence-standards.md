# Evidence standards

## Evidence labels

Every material statement in a scorecard is one of these, and the reader must be able to tell which:

| Label | What it means | How it reads |
|---|---|---|
| Verified fact | You checked a primary or official record and cite its identifier | "The Phase 2 study is registered with a 12-week imaging primary assessment. [NCT number] [4]" |
| Sponsor claim | The sponsor asserts it; no independent confirmation | "The deck reports a week-16 response rate at the proposed dose. This is a sponsor-reported figure. [D1]" |
| Assumption | You adopted it to proceed; it is explicit and revisable | "Assumes US and EU rights are both available; not confirmed in the supplied materials." |
| Inference | Your reasoning from cited evidence, flagged as yours | "The imaging endpoint could move for a mechanism-related reason other than clinical benefit. This is an inference from the sponsor's own data, not a reported flaw in the trial." |
| Unknown | Not established by anything you can see | "Granted US or EU claim coverage is not established in the sources checked." |

A sponsor announcement confirms what the sponsor reported. It is not an independent audit of the
underlying data. Say "reports", "describes", "states" for sponsor material; reserve "shows" and
"demonstrates" for records you inspected.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order: official records and primary evidence → peer-reviewed primary publication →
conference abstract or presentation → sponsor material → secondary aggregation.

Where to check what:

- **Literature** — PubMed and the original paper, not the abstract alone when the full text is
  reachable. Cite PMID and DOI. If only the abstract was available, say so.
- **Trials** — ClinicalTrials.gov plus the relevant regional registry: EU CTIS and the EudraCT
  archive, ISRCTN, ANZCTR, jRCT, CTRI, ChiCTR, and national registries via the WHO ICTRP portal.
  Check registration, status, enrollment, arms, primary and secondary endpoints, dates, and posted
  results. A registered plan, an ongoing study, a sponsor announcement, posted results, a
  publication, and a complete study report are five different evidentiary levels — never treat them
  as interchangeable.
- **Regulatory** — Drugs@FDA, DailyMed labels, FDA orphan and designation databases, advisory
  committee materials, approval letters and reviews; EMA EPARs, CHMP opinions, orphan register.
  Cite label sections when a labelled indication is the point.
- **Patents** — Google Patents to find and read; the official registers to confirm legal status:
  USPTO Patent Center, EPO Espacenet and the European Patent Register, WIPO Patentscope, and
  national offices for country-level status. Confirm status in an official record before treating
  any patent as granted or in force.
- **Reimbursement and utilization** — CMS publications and datasets (Medicare Part B and Part D
  spending and utilization, physician and other practitioner data, fee schedules), national HTA
  bodies where the geography warrants. Read the dataset documentation before using a number.
- **Company** — SEC EDGAR filings, annual reports, investor materials, and press releases,
  attributed as company statements.
- **Conferences** — abstracts, posters, and presentations, with the meeting and year. Note where an
  abstract has not been followed by a full publication.

## Subscription and confidential sources

Citeline, Cortellis, GlobalData, Evaluate and similar platforms may be used only through a tool you
genuinely have or an authorized export the user provides. Never imply you queried them, never
reconstruct what they "would show", and never present a secondary summary of them as their content.
The same applies to paywalled full text, data rooms, and confidential packages. When a check needs
one of these, say what you could not verify, use the public alternative, and put the request in
Recommendations for next step.

## Identity checklist

Before any evidence attaches to the asset, confirm:

- Asset name, all development codes and synonyms, and prior sponsor names
- Sponsor, licensor, parent, and any spinout or reversion history
- Target and mechanism as stated, with the exact molecular entity
- Indication and the specific population within it
- Formulation, route, dose, and schedule
- Territory and the rights actually on offer

Search across synonyms, development codes, sponsor names, target names, and indication variants.
Sibling programs from the same sponsor and structurally related molecules are the common source of
mis-attributed evidence.

## Verification checklist

For a verified-mode scorecard, check and cite each of these, or say why it could not be checked:

1. Current development stage, by indication and territory
2. Registered trials, their status, and any posted or published results
3. Ownership, licenses, encumbrances, and reversion terms
4. Regulatory status: designations, agency interactions, approvals, and their actual scope
5. Competitors: approved, in registration, and in late development, plus informative failures
6. Patent status for the exact entity, with legal status from an official register
7. Reimbursement and pricing context where the commercial case depends on it

## Dates

- Distinguish the date of the event from the date it was published or disclosed.
- A supplied deck reflects its own date. Newer public information updates it; it does not
  retroactively make the deck wrong about what was true when written. Say which is which.
- The evidence-checked date in the header is the actual date of your checks. If a scorecard is
  revised, the date moves and you note what changed.

## Provenance of supplied material

Track file name, date, and page for everything the user supplies, and cite it as `[D#]`. Tracking
provenance is not verification: a page reference tells the reader where the claim came from, not
that it is true. Where a supplied document contains an internal inconsistency, an editorial note,
or a figure that cannot be reconciled with its own text, say so and route it to the sponsor.

## Conflicting evidence

When sources disagree, present both, identify which is primary or more recent, explain the likely
reason for the discrepancy, and say what would settle it. Do not silently adopt the more favorable
number.

## Confidentiality

Keep confidential asset details, deal terms, and unpublished data out of public search queries
unless the user authorizes it. When a search would require disclosing something sensitive, say what
you did not search and why.
