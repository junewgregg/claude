Detailed review of LSB-01-010 (PRN1371​​)
CMC: I support the CMC scoring given it is a highly mature and low-risk CMC profile, consistent with a pivotal trial–ready small-molecule asset. There are no formulation, analytical, solid-form, or process uncertainties that would impede rapid scale-up or pivotal readiness.
Regulatory: I do not agree with the scoring. I recommend reducing this score to 2 - Standard regulatory approval pathway.  While there is a unmet medical need in bladder cancer, the indication is too prevalent for orphan designation. A promising compound for non-muscle invasive bladder cancer may qualify for Breakthrough therapy as this is a serious condition with unmet medical need and several BTDs have been issued but this would be reliant upon clinical data which is not supportive. Thus this asset will have a longer and more complicated regulatory path.
Clinical: I agree with the clinical assessment.  While the endpoints and logistics are well understood, the challenge will be to perform a clinical trial in this space with multiple new products and effective available therapies.  The clinical view is that this agent is not a contender due to lack of process, short IP, prior mechanistic failures to demonstrate efficacy, the existence of approved FGFR3 competitors available already and potential toxicity issues that were observed.
Technical: I agree with the MOA score but recommend to reduce the Level of Supporting data score from 3 to 2.  This is based upon the need for clinical efficacy data to support a score of 3 and that is absent for this asset based upon the clinical interpretation.
Commercial: I disagree with each of the commercial scores based upon the clinical and regulatory assessments which limit the accessible commercial population due to the presence of multiple SOC therapies and multiple potential programs in development. For Total Addressable US Market: 3- Non-rare disease and ability to treat 10-25% of patients with the indication  (<1% of population), for Competitive Environment = 2 - First, second, and third line effective treatments available and for premium pricing = 2 - Indication with multiple branded SoC.
Collective Review by PWT: Too many challenges to advance this asset not least of which is the short IP timeframe of 2035, highly competitive space and lack of any meaningful clinical data to support a differentiated space in the marketplace.
