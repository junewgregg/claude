# Second Review — LSB-02-173 · IACS-16559 (MD Anderson)
**Panel Determination: Prioritize**
**Analyst Rating: Diligence → Panel Upgrade to Prioritize**

## Asset Overview
IACS-16559 is an oral, once-daily, highly selective small-molecule inhibitor of the CBP/EP300 bromodomains, developed at MD Anderson Cancer Center. It is designed to disrupt MYC/MYB-driven oncogenic transcription programs in genetically defined AML subtypes. GLP tox complete, CMC via Wuxi advanced, IND submission targeted in ~3 months at time of review.

## Why It Cleared for Prioritize

**Development readiness is unusually high for an academic asset.** GLP 28-day rat and dog tox completed. Manufacturing run via Wuxi complete. IND written. Clinical-grade supply ready. This substantially de-risks the typical academic-spin-out execution risk.

**Biomarker-driven strategy is credible.** The program targets AML patients with menin/KAT2A aberrations — a defined genomic subpopulation. Preclinical synergy with approved menin inhibitors (revumenib, ziftomenib) provides a clear combination hypothesis and a plausible regulatory path (add-on to SOC).

**Selectivity advantage over lead competitor.** IACS-16559 shows higher selectivity for P300/CBP vs. BRD4 relative to inobrodib (CellCentric), the furthest-advanced competitor. Clean HERG/CYP profile unlike inobrodib is a meaningful differentiation point for clinical development.

**MD Anderson AML infrastructure.** Single-site Phase 1 feasibility (~14 months projected) is realistic given MDACC's patient volume and AML expertise. This shortens the time-to-data significantly.

## Panel Swing Factor
The combination of near-clinical-stage readiness (tox done, CMC done, IND ready), a defined genomic patient selection hypothesis, and competitive differentiation on selectivity was decisive. The panel viewed this as actionable rather than exploratory.

## Risks Noted
- CBP/P300 class is associated with potential hematotoxicity — therapeutic window in AML (fragile marrow reserve) requires careful dose-finding
- Competitive landscape is evolving rapidly: inobrodib (CellCentric) has Phase 2 ASH data; PROTAC/degrader approaches may leapfrog bromodomain inhibitors
- Monotherapy efficacy in AML is uncertain; combination development adds regulatory complexity
- Overall development plan somewhat unclear; combination sequencing (with menin inhibitors) needs to be locked before IND

## Reviewer Consensus
Five reviewers, strongly convergent. Reviewers 1-4 all identified the CBP/P300 mechanism, selectivity advantage, and AML biomarker strategy as positive. Common concern: combination-vs-monotherapy path. Reviewer 5 (likely most clinical) summarized the investment case most tightly: MD Anderson infrastructure, 14-month Phase 1, CMC done, clean tox, competitive differentiation vs. inobrodib.

## Panel vs. Analyst Divergence
Analyst might rate Diligence given the lack of human data. Panel upgraded to Prioritize based on the near-clinical readiness, quality of the development team (MDACC), and the concreteness of the biomarker strategy — factors that reduce execution risk substantially below a typical preclinical academic program.
