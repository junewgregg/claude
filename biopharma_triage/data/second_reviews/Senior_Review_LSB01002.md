Detailed review of LSB-01-002 (ACM-CpG​)
CMC: I support the CMC scoring given the significant prior human work, the use of established partners for oligos, and the well-defined manufacturing process. Though there are some significant challenges remaining most notably related to analytics, the CMC manufacturing process does not appear to be limiting for this asset.
Regulatory: I do not agree with the scoring. The score given was Regulatory Path: ​5 - Early or Expedited approval opportunity (e.g. prior to Ph3 or skip Ph 2) under the assumption of gaining orphan or accelerated approval for the chosen indication​.  The asset has completed a safety only study in Australia.  Some ADME challenges were noted related to immunogenicity. Breakthrough/RMAT designations are dependent upon being superior to standard of care for which the clinical assessment indicates that will be unlikely given several TLR9 approaches in advanced development. Consequently, I recommend reducing the regulatory score to 3 - Orphan / Breakthrough / Fast Track Approval possible.
Clinical: I agree with the clinical assessment.  The clinical assessment for this asset is highly negative and the clinical assessor is an expert in both the asset category (LNP-CpG and oncology).  ACM-CpG is one of many TLR9 agonists, including several packaged in LNP delivery vehicles, that are undergoing clinical development in a variety of settings in an effort to stimulate immunity.   The challenge is to gain POC of clinical benefits offered in the chosen indication via local administration and its impact on widely systemic metastatic disease. The indication, appendiceal carcinomatosis is a rare tumor, creating layers of logistical, operation, and commercial issues.  Consequently, the clinical success of this asset is scored as having low potential.
Technical: The technical review is appropriate for the MOA rationale and the level of evidence supporting this asset.
Commercial: I agree with two aspects of the commercial review related to “addressable market” and potential for “premium pricing”.  I do not agree with the score of 5 for Competitive Environment based upon the current No FDA Approved treatment options available​.  This statement fails to acknowledge the multiple advanced TLR9 programs in development, some of which will reach the market prior to LSB-01-002 (ACM-CpG​).  Thus, I recommend reducing to 3 - First and second line effective treatments available.
Collective Review by PWT: LSB-01-002 is a classic example of a “platform technology” looking for a disease target and happening on TLR9, given the existence of multiple advanced TLR9 development programs.  The program lacks the requisite expertise in TLR9 targeting and development and is absent POC as monotherapy or when added to standard-of-care regimens.
