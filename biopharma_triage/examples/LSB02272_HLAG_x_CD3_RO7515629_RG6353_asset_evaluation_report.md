| Executive conclusion |
Lead asset framing: First-generation HLA-G T-cell engager targeting immune-suppressive solid tumors, discontinued by Roche after very limited clinical dosing despite a broad preclinical package.
Why it lands at Deprioritize: The asset has clear biological rationale and attractive target prevalence, but only three patients were dosed and the company already chose to stop the program for strategic reasons, leaving too much clinical and translational uncertainty.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: If revived, HLA-G-high CPI-experienced RCC or PDAC would be the cleanest first populations.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: Combination with 4-1BBL or other immune-activating partners is conceptually appealing but premature for underwriting.

| Evidence review |
Class rationale: HLA-G is an immune-suppressive antigen with limited normal-tissue expression and broad prevalence in several solid tumors.
Differentiation claim: target choice is novel versus more crowded solid-tumor TCE spaces, and monotherapy/combination preclinical activity is supportive.
Lead-indication evidence: the actual clinical package is minimal - only three patients dosed at low levels before the program was stopped.
Combination logic: preclinical combination activity exists, but this is not yet a reason to spend screening resources now.
Expansion-indication evidence: broad prevalence is interesting but actually worsens focus risk at the present stage.
