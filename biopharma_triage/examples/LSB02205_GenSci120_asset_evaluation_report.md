Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: GenSci120 is a PD-1 receptor agonistic, PD-1high cell-depleting IgG1 monoclonal antibody aimed at pathogenic T-cell populations across autoimmune disease.
Why it lands at Diligence: The Diligence rating reflects unusually strong mechanistic coherence for an early autoimmune program, with both animal efficacy and early human target-engagement/depletion data. Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: SLE and pSS expand upside once the initial autoimmunity thesis is de-risked, but they should not lead the first path.

Evidence review
Class rationale: Targeting PD-1-high pathogenic T cells is an attractive immune-reset concept that could be more selective than broad immune suppression if the biology holds in patients.
Differentiation claim: The sponsor positions GenSci120 as differentiated from rosnilimab/peresolimab through membrane-proximal epitope binding, stronger agonism, selective depletion, ligand-enhancing properties, and longer half-life.
Lead-indication evidence: Preclinical models in CIA arthritis and acute colitis show disease improvement, while FIH healthy-volunteer data show favorable tolerability, ~20-day half-life, full PD-1+ T-cell saturation, and selective depletion of PD-1high T cells—strong biological groundwork but not clinical efficacy.
Combination logic: Combination is not the primary value proposition; the aim is disease control through selective pathogenic T-cell suppression and agonism.
Expansion-indication evidence: Broad autoimmune applicability is plausible but should remain secondary until one lead patient dataset emerges.

What broadening too early would risk: Pursuing too many autoimmune indications at once would burn capital and blur the fastest path to clinical proof.

Lead-indication selection memo and proposed Phase II design, endpoints, and biomarker plan.
Competitive differentiation analysis versus rosnilimab/peresolimab and current autoimmune biologics/JAKs.
