Screening view: Etomoxir is an on-priority oncology small molecule with official FDA orphan designation in malignant glioma, a coherent mitochondrial-fatty-acid-oxidation thesis, and meaningful historical human exposure. It stays below Acquire because all efficacy evidence in glioma is still preclinical and the old CHF program exposed a real transaminase liability that must be re-underwritten in oncology.
Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: Etomoxir is a first-in-class CPT1 inhibitor being repositioned by Numiera Therapeutics into malignant glioma / glioblastoma using an orphan-neuro-oncology strategy, a repurposing-oriented regulatory thesis, and an early window-of-opportunity surgical design.
Why it lands at Diligence: The asset lands at Diligence / Hold because disease fit and modality fit are both strong, the target is expressed broadly across human GBM samples, and the sponsor has assembled a tangible Phase 1 design with liver/cardiac monitoring and tissue PK/PD. The gating issue is that prior human data came from congestive heart failure, not oncology, and that program was stopped after high liver transaminases in four etomoxir-treated patients.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: Recurrent or progressive malignant glioma / GBM patients, especially those eligible for the planned window-of-opportunity surgical cohort where direct tumor exposure and pharmacodynamic proof can be obtained early.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: If human brain-tumor PK/PD and safety cooperate, the CPT1 thesis could expand into biomarker-defined treatment-resistant breast, prostate, or bladder settings, but that expansion should not dilute the first malignant-glioma proof path.

Regulatory path, commercial framing, and thesis test
Execution status: More real than a typical IND-enabling repurposing pitch: the sponsor shows a concrete clinical schedule, specific monitoring burdens, and a surgical proof-of-mechanism cohort. The execution burden is still nontrivial because safety monitoring is central, not peripheral.
Most coherent first commercial / clinical story: The most coherent first story is a recurrent malignant-glioma orphan path that uses surgery-enabled tumor sampling to answer the questions that matter most: brain exposure, target engagement, and whether the safety window is workable.
What broadening too early would risk: Broadening into breast, prostate, or bladder too early would turn a sharp orphan-neuro-oncology path into a diffuse metabolism platform story before the central human risk question is answered.

Key risks, diligence questions, and out-of-rating watchouts
Historical hepatotoxicity is not a footnote; it is the central diligence risk and must be bridged against proposed oncology dosing/exposure.
No direct human oncology efficacy data exist yet, so translational risk remains high despite elegant preclinical biology.
The sponsor’s 505(b)(2) framing may overstate how much old non-oncology safety can practically compress an oncology orphan path.
Priority diligence requests
Full oncology-enabling toxicology and dose-selection package, including the bridge from historical CHF exposures to the planned glioma regimen and stopping rules for AST/ALT, troponin, and ECG findings.
Regulatory correspondence or advisor memos clarifying whether the 505(b)(2) thesis is realistic for malignant glioma and what prior etomoxir data are actually bridgeable.
Operational plan and statistical assumptions for the surgical window cohort, including tissue-PK assay readiness and criteria for escalating into broader expansion cohorts.
Rating-movement conditions and bottom-line recommendation
Move to Acquire if: FDA and IND-enabling work confirm a usable hepatic/cardiac safety window and the surgical cohort shows measurable tumor exposure / PD in glioma tissue.
Move to Acquire if: Early recurrent-glioma clinical data show credible radiographic stabilization or progression-slowing signal beyond what would be expected from historical controls alone.
Move to Deprioritize if: Liver or cardiac liability reappears at exposures needed for pharmacology, collapsing the central repurposing thesis.
Move to Deprioritize if: Surgical PK/PD fails to show adequate tumor exposure or target engagement, leaving no persuasive bridge from preclinical efficacy to the clinic.
Bottom line: DILIGENCE / HOLD. Etomoxir deserves an actual review package now that the orphan designation, preclinical glioma evidence, and concrete Phase 1 design are on the table. But it still sits in the yellow bucket because the asset is not yet clinically real in oncology and the historical transaminase signal could still be a program-killing issue.
