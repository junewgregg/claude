| Executive conclusion |
Lead asset framing: Clinically active GPRC5D/CD3 bispecific for heavily pretreated RRMM, discontinued by Roche for strategic reasons rather than an obvious efficacy failure.
Why it lands at Diligence: Response rates, durability and activity in high-risk subgroups are clearly attractive. The gating issues are full-package verification, manageable chronic tolerability in a competitive class, and capital/commercial demands in post-BCMA myeloma.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: Triple-class refractory and post-BCMA RRMM patients needing a non-BCMA T-cell engager with durable responses.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: Combination with carfilzomib and route/dose optimization could add value once monotherapy posture is fully diligence-graded.

| Evidence review |
Class rationale: GPRC5D is a validated myeloma antigen with limited normal-tissue distribution and meaningful post-BCMA relevance.
Differentiation claim: Roche highlights a novel 2:1 format, strong activity, and route optimization with IV/SC dosing.
Lead-indication evidence: phase I disclosed high ORR and durable responses across all-comer and difficult-to-treat subgroups, including prior BCMA exposure.
Combination logic: combination study exists, but monotherapy and dosing/route optimization remain the key diligence focus.
Expansion-indication evidence: within MM, refractory subgroups are likely the clearest first-value segment.
