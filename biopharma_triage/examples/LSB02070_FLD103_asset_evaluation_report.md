Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: FLD-103 is Feldan’s intralesional antisense oligonucleotide against GLI1 for basal cell carcinoma, enabled by the proprietary Feldan Shuttle intracellular-delivery platform.
Why it lands at Diligence: The asset earns a Diligence call because there is a real non-surgical need in cosmetically sensitive BCC, first-patient-in is public, and the development plan is capital-light relative to many oncology assets. It remains below Acquire because LSB priority fit is only adjacent and because clinical clearance rates versus surgery remain unproven.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: Nodular BCC in cosmetically sensitive face/head/neck lesions where patients and dermatologic surgeons value tissue preservation, reduced scarring, and an office-based alternative to Mohs.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: Potential use in multiple-lesion patients, Gorlin syndrome, or neoadjuvant tumor downsizing could add value after an initial nodular BCC proof point.

Evidence review
Class rationale: Targeting GLI1 downstream of canonical and non-canonical Hedgehog signaling is mechanistically sound for BCC and may address escape routes that limit upstream pathway targeting.
Differentiation claim: Differentiation is non-surgical local treatment of nodular BCC with potentially better cosmetic burden than Mohs, plus avoidance of systemic HHI toxicities.
Lead-indication evidence: Sponsor materials position a >$3.75B addressable U.S. market with 2.5M nodular BCC cases and emphasize routine use of injectable techniques among Mohs surgeons; public company disclosure also confirms first-patient dosing in the Phase 1/2a study.
Combination logic: Combination is not the first story; workflow fit with dermatologic procedures matters more than oncology combination logic.
Expansion-indication evidence: Expansion into neoadjuvant or multi-lesion settings is plausible but should come after lesion clearance and cosmetic outcome data are credible.

Regulatory path, commercial framing, and thesis test
Execution status: Clinical execution appears focused and relatively capital-light, with funding through Q1 2027 to deliver Phase 1/2a data.
Most coherent first commercial / clinical story: Best story is not replacement of every BCC surgery; it is selective uptake in facial or otherwise cosmetically sensitive lesions where patients and surgeons seek a non-surgical option.
What broadening too early would risk: Broadening too early into generic BCC or additional dermatology uses would weaken the highly specific adoption thesis.

Key risks, diligence questions, and out-of-rating watchouts
Clinical efficacy may not approach the lesion control expected from Mohs/excision.
Injection logistics and repeat visits could limit dermatologist uptake even with good biology.
RNA delivery advantage is still platform-claim-heavy relative to human outcome data.
Priority diligence requests
Protocol and current enrollment status for the Phase 1/2a BCC study, including lesion subtype mix.
Planned efficacy endpoints: histologic clearance, cosmetic outcome, recurrence follow-up, and procedural burden.
Detailed local tolerability and retreatment assumptions, plus payer/adoption work with Mohs surgeons and community dermatology.
Rating-movement conditions and bottom-line recommendation
Move to Acquire if: Phase 1/2a shows strong histologic or durable lesion clearance with clearly favorable cosmetic/procedural profile in nodular BCC.
Move to Acquire if: Adoption work confirms dermatologists can integrate the injection workflow without materially disrupting practice economics.
Move to Deprioritize if: Efficacy falls materially short of surgical expectations or requires cumbersome repeat dosing.
Move to Deprioritize if: The platform remains interesting scientifically but the lead commercial use case proves too narrow or operationally awkward.
Bottom line: DILIGENCE. FLD-103 is a focused, clinically active asset with a credible unmet-need narrative and a practical first use case in nodular BCC. It does not clear Acquire because it sits only adjacent to LSB’s priority framework and still needs to prove procedure-relevant efficacy and real-world workflow fit. The next decision point is the Phase 1/2a readout, not a broader platform extrapolation.
