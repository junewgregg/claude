Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: GenSci143 is a B7-H3 × PSMA bispecific Topo1 ADC intended to broaden coverage across heterogeneous mCRPC and overcome the response limitations of single-target PSMA therapies.
Why it lands at Diligence: mCRPC is strategically relevant, the dual-target rationale is coherent, and the molecule appears differentiated on binding, internalization, and plasma stability. It remains below Acquire because the current diligence package does not show human efficacy, and the exact clinical maturity is not yet clean.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: mCRPC patients with heterogeneous PSMA expression, especially those less likely to benefit uniformly from purely PSMA-dependent approaches.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: NSCLC, SCLC, and endometrial cancer are optionality stories that should wait until prostate proof is established.

Evidence review
Class rationale: Dual targeting of B7-H3 and PSMA could address heterogeneous antigen expression and improve coverage versus radioligand or single-target ADC strategies.
Differentiation claim: GenSci claims near-all-comer coverage in mCRPC (either-or-both antigen expression), superior internalization and binding versus benchmark analogs, and much better plasma stability than DS-7300/DS-8201 analogs.
Lead-indication evidence: The sponsor shows ~97% of prostate cancers express high levels of either or both B7-H3 and PSMA, plus better internalization, binding, and plasma stability than comparator analogs. Those are attractive but still preclinical supporting points.
Combination logic: Could later combine with androgen-pathway or other standard therapies, but first the molecule must prove its standalone targeted coverage story.
Expansion-indication evidence: Expansion into NSCLC/SCLC/endometrial cancer is real optionality but should not affect the first go/no-go decision.
