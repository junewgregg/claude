Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: GenSci128 is a small-molecule TP53 Y220C reactivator for biomarker-selected solid tumors, positioned as potentially best-in-class on potency, exposure efficiency, and safety window.
Why it lands at Diligence: The package is good enough to keep active because target biology is clinically validated, biomarker selection is straightforward, and GenSci shows encouraging preclinical and early proof-of-mechanism data. It remains below Acquire because the market will likely benchmark GenSci128 directly against PMV’s PC14586/rezatapopt, which is further along clinically.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: KRAS-wild-type TP53 Y220C ovarian or breast cancer patients, where competitor activity has already made the class clinically legible and biomarker-driven enrollment is practical.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: Pancreatic, gastric, and lung tumors with Y220C mutations create broader tumor-agnostic upside after the first signal is established.

Evidence review
Class rationale: p53 Y220C is a rare but clinically validated hotspot mutation; structural reactivation is one of the more compelling mutation-corrector stories in oncology.
Differentiation claim: GenSci claims 3-4x stronger potency, comparable/better in vivo efficacy at 1/10–1/4 competitor exposure, and a wider toxicology safety window than PC14586.
Lead-indication evidence: The deck shows preliminary proof-of-mechanism in patients via dose-dependent GDF15 increase and strong preclinical tumor-growth inhibition at lower exposure; externally, PMV has reported a 38% confirmed ORR at RP2D for PC14586 in TP53 Y220C/KRAS WT patients, which validates the class but raises the benchmark.
Combination logic: Combination could eventually matter, but the immediate job is to prove GenSci128 can stand on its own against the leading class comparator.
Expansion-indication evidence: Tumor-agnostic expansion is plausible because the biomarker is the organizing principle, not the tissue alone.
