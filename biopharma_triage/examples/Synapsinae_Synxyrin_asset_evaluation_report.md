| Executive conclusion |
Lead asset framing: Next-generation NMDA modulators positioned as balanced alternatives to antagonists like esketamine/dextromethorphan, with Synapsinae furthest advanced in major depression.
Why it lands at Diligence: The core appeal is oral CNS differentiation with registration-enabling readiness, but the uploaded deck provides limited direct efficacy detail in the current package and the disease fit is only adjacent to LSB's preferred neuroscience focus.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: Patients with MDD who need a better-tolerated oral alternative to dissociative or abuse-liability-prone NMDA antagonists; Synxyrin fits more refractory disease.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: Broader dementia assets increase platform value, but the cleanest first screening lane is major depression.

| Evidence review |
Class rationale: NMDA-system modulation is a validated antidepressant axis; SyneuRx argues for balanced modulation rather than strong antagonism.
Differentiation claim: the sponsor positions lower network instability, dissociation and abuse risk versus antagonists, but head-to-head human evidence is not yet shown in this package.
Lead-indication evidence: the deck emphasizes Phase IIb/III readiness for Synapsinae and Phase II readiness for Synxyrin more than it discloses a diligence-grade efficacy dataset.
Combination logic: the cleaner commercial story is oral monotherapy or oral adjunctive use after SSRI/SNRI failure.
Expansion-indication evidence: dementia optionality adds strategic upside but should not be underwritten from the depression screen.
