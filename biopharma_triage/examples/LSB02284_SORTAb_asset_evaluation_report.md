Executive conclusion
Lead asset framing: Eygo Bio is developing an intravitreal anti-sortilin antibody for geographic atrophy, aiming to pair neuroprotection with anti-inflammatory effects and lower treatment burden than current approaches.
Why it lands at Deprioritize: The package is polished, the biology is interesting, and the stated capital / timeline are comparatively reasonable; however, the lead disease area is off-priority for the current LSB framework, so the default outcome is deprioritization rather than deeper action now.
Best-fit entry indication and patient population: Patients with geographic atrophy secondary to AMD where slowing lesion growth and preserving function could differentiate from current complement-focused approaches.
Commercially attractive but not first-line screening focus: There may be longer-term retinal neuroprotection optionality in glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy, but those opportunities do not change the current screen result.

Evidence review
Class rationale: The package links sortilin biology to retinal apoptosis and inflammation, creating a coherent multimodal neuroprotection thesis for GA.
Differentiation claim: Eygo argues that sortilin inhibition could combine neuroprotection and anti-inflammation with a relatively low dosing burden and without the wet-AMD concern associated with current therapies.
Lead-indication evidence: The most concrete efficacy support is preclinical: a mouse NaIO3 model showing improved retinal structure and lower inflammatory-cell burden using an anti-sortilin tool antibody.
Combination logic: The story is mainly monotherapy positioning rather than a combination-dependent thesis, which is a positive for execution simplicity.
Expansion-indication evidence: Glaucoma and diabetic retinopathy provide optionality, but remain exploratory extensions rather than near-term value drivers.

Key risks, diligence questions, and out-of-rating watchouts
The core efficacy support is still preclinical and relies in part on a tool antibody rather than disclosed human-clinical evidence.
Meaningful functional vision benefit remains unproven.
The asset is outside current LSB disease priorities, which constrains near-term attention regardless of technical merit.
Priority diligence requests:
Lead-specific IND-enabling data, ocular tolerability package, and translational biomarker plan.
Evidence linking structural rescue to meaningful functional endpoints and payer-relevant differentiation.
A sharper view on whether any broader retinal-neuroprotection thesis could connect to future strategic priority areas.
Missing information / out-of-rating watchouts: Current uploaded materials are teaser-level rather than full diligence packs. IP depth, sponsor financing, full CMC status, ownership complexity, and detailed clinical datasets remain incomplete and should inform diligence scope, not the headline rating.
Rating-movement conditions and bottom-line recommendation
Move to Acquire if: The strategic priority framework broadens to include ophthalmology / retinal neuroprotection, or the asset demonstrates unusually compelling cross-disease relevance.
Move to Acquire if: Lead-specific IND-enabling data and functional efficacy logic substantially de-risk the first-in-class story.
Move to Deprioritize if: IND-enabling progress slips or the efficacy case remains structural only without a credible functional-vision thesis.
Move to Deprioritize if: Competing GA approaches continue to outpace the program while the disease area remains outside core priorities.
Bottom line: Deprioritize. Eygo has built a credible and commercially attractive ophthalmology story around a differentiated mechanism, and the package is stronger than many early-stage decks. But under the current LSB framework, geographic atrophy is not a priority disease area, so this is not a good use of near-term diligence bandwidth. The right posture is to keep it on the radar, not to lean in now.
